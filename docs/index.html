I am Professor of Sociology in the Core Program at Kadir Has University, Istanbul. I received my PhD in sociology from the University of Washington in 2009 and taught at Boğaziçi University from 2010 to 2026. My main fields are sociological methods, quantitative research methods, urban sociology, economic sociology, nationalism and the sociology of religion. For the past two years, my work has focused mostly on understanding and explaining the cultural values and beliefs of people in Turkey, and how they change.

Current research

My research has always been driven by causal questions. For the first half of my career, I pursued them through qualitative methods and small-n comparisons. Over time, I grew skeptical that such comparisons can answer them reliably, and I moved to quantitative methods. Today I use large-scale surveys with embedded experiments, analyzed in R, to map the cultural attitudes and beliefs of people in Turkey, and I work on models that identify which social factors shape them. This shift shapes both my current projects and the way I teach.

The largest of these projects, which I am carrying out together with my collaborator Volkan Ertit, is a multi-university study of secularity, belief types and cultural attitudes among university students in Turkey. It covers 50 universities and a sample of close to 36,000 students. The study applies a secularity scale to measure students’ secularity, compares these levels across demographic groups, asks which factors best predict secularity, and examines how groups differ on key cultural attitudes. The study is supported by a grant from Boğaziçi University’s Scientific Research Projects (BAP) program.

Before this, I studied nationalist sentiments in Turkey with a sample of about 1,500 respondents. Most research on nationalism is historical or small-n, so a study like this is rather unconventional. My co-author Turgut Keskintürk and I measured nationalist sentiments along five dimensions and used latent class analysis to identify four distinct groups. A resource distribution task in the survey asked people to divide limited economic resources between in-group and out-group members, which let us examine how nationalist attitudes relate to fairness. This project was also supported by a BAP grant. The results are published in Nations and Nationalism.

My next project will bring my work on nationalism and religiosity-secularity together. Focusing again on university students, I will examine how nationalism and religiosity intersect, and how this intersection shapes attitudes such as fairness. I will also run experiments within the survey, randomly assigning participants to different conditions, so that I can address these causal questions more directly.

Earlier work

Earlier in my career, I spent many years studying urban regeneration and renewal in Turkey, mostly through qualitative research and small-n comparisons. In Istanbul, I asked why renewal projects failed despite strong state backing and favorable macroeconomic conditions. In six other cities, I studied the regeneration of former Sümerbank factory sites to understand why some of these initiatives failed while others succeeded. This work appeared in Urban Studies, International Journal of Urban and Regional Research, Urban Affairs Review and Urban Geography.

Teaching

Teaching has always been the backbone of my academic career. I feel a responsibility to my students, and I firmly believe that one learns best by teaching.

I particularly enjoy teaching Introduction to Sociology, a course offered to students from every major. It helps them develop a sociological perspective, both theoretical and methodological, whatever they end up studying. Over the years, I have also taught Urban Sociology, Social Change and Development, and Sociology of Finance.

Recently, teaching methods sequences, at both undergraduate and graduate levels, has taken center stage in my teaching career because it is methods that make a science! In the coming years, I plan to build on my methods courses to create a computational social science sequence that trains students in R.
